Dismantle Mercury!

[2026/10/30] · futurism

Mercury has always been my least favourite planet. I used to study astrophysics, so I speak on this topic with no more authority than anyone else. It's just a bit drab compared to the wondrous beauty of the gas giants, the shining poisons of Venus, and, of course, Earth's life. (Mars is alright, I guess.)

But there is one thing that draws my attention to Mercury. I've likely given it far more thought than most planets, in fact.

It's fantastic Dyson swarm material! Many astrophysicists believe it had a strong collision sometime in its history, meaning that it is mostly core. In other words, its core is way closer to the surface than other planets (both in relative and absolute terms) and comparatively easy to access. And do you know what that core is made of? Iron! Along with some other stuff.

So you get solar-powered machines on Mercury to begin taking Mercury apart to build more machines to take Mercury apart. As Mercury gets less massive, it gets easier to launch solar panels and mirrors.

I know I said Mercury is my least favourite planet, but there is something a little sad about actually dismantling (like robbing it of its mantle, haha) it. The Solar System will always have been humanity's first star system. I do think it is wildly, absolutely worth it though. Mercury being so close to the Sun, iron-rich, and mostly core is as awesome for us as the Moon being the same size as the Sun from Earth's vantage point. We should put this incredible advantage to good use! As Benji describes it, "We got the tutorial system."

This is such a popular concept already, but I am writing about it to help illustrate my preferences more, as I haven't done too much of that yet. I've realised that in my head I have lots of things that I think of as "Oh, everyone has said this already," but how are people supposed to know that I hold this viewpoint unless I talk about it?
