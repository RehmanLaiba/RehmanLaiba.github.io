Hello! My name is Laiba Rehman. My main focus right now is AI safety, namely trying to make sure AI doesn't kill everybody.

How I Got Here

Go to AI Safety Work if you want to skip over the personal stuff.

The way I got here is very salient to me. I used to be very religious; specifically I was a pious Muslim. I had been doubting religion for a long time, but it all came crashing down with finality on the 1st of September, 2025, when I proclaimed myself an atheist.

My number one worry was death. I used to believe that, if I were Good enough, I'd get to go to Jannah (Heaven), and the same went for everyone else. (Being morally good inherently meant being a good Muslim. I didn't believe this on an emotional level, but told myself this to keep things consistent.) I was studying astrophysics at the undergraduate level, with plans to eventually because an astrophysics professor, but after becoming an atheist I decided I was going to pivot into some sort of anti-death work, either cryonics or longevity or something else. I started studying biology textbooks alongside my course with the intent to do a master's that would help me transition into the field of fighting death.

However, I then read the book, If Anyone Builds It, Everyone Dies. This started a deepening interest and worry about the existential risk of AI. My worry only grew over the months as I realised how little time we had. It got to a point where I could hardly bear doing any physics, as it felt like throwing what little time we had left away. I decided to take a gap year with the expectation of fully dropping out at some point in order to focus on AI safety, and I'm so glad I did.

The importance of mentioning my apostasy will likely fade over time (if I live long enough) as I get more experience between leaving Islam and that point in time, but right now it's pretty much the only honest way I can describe how I got here and why I'm doing AI safety. I want to be clear that there are many others who agree with me on AI safety but are religious themselves, and that just because it was a necessary part of my journey to dedicating my life to preventing human extinction, this is absolutely not a prerequisite for everyone else.
